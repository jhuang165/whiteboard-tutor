# Oral reasoning practice

Adapted from Matt Pocock's grill-me/grilling workflow for course reasoning.
Read `CLAUDE.md`, the argument or topic, and the relevant source. Use existing
context to choose a starting point; look up source facts directly when needed.

Map the argument's assumptions and dependent claims. Ask one focused question
whose answer determines the next branch. Useful probes include: why a denominator
describes the conditioning event; when a step requires independence; whether a
counterexample breaks a generalization; how changing the sampling mechanism
changes the result. Wait for the learner's answer before choosing the next probe.

Probe rigorously and keep the tone constructive. Do not reveal the preferred
answer inside the question. When the learner is stuck, offer a small hint or
explanation and distinguish assisted from independent reasoning. Honor requests
to change direction, get the full explanation, or stop.

Finish with the claims that held up, the specific unresolved gap, and one useful
next exercise. Checkpoint learning evidence through the shared-memory bridge.
